> **⚠️ SUPERSEDED (2026-08-31).** Early, narrower "reflection-partner" framing. Product scope is now the
> full LifeOS — see `docs/ATLAS_BIBLE.md` §1–§3. Kept for historical context only.

Builds on ATLAS_PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md. This document defines the exact first version to build — nothing more.

## 1. Core User Promise

*"Capture a little about your life, and Atlas will show you the pattern you couldn't see yourself."*

## 2. First-Time User Experience (Signup → First Insight)

1. **Signup** — email/password or SSO. No onboarding survey wall before value; keep friction minimal.
2. **Seed reflection (5-7 questions, ~3 minutes)** — current goals, current struggles, a recent decision they're unsure about, what they want to understand about themselves. This gives the AI system context before any entries exist, so it isn't starting cold.
3. **First capture prompt** — immediately after seeding, ask for one real entry ("What's on your mind right now?") so the user experiences the capture flow before leaving the app.
4. **Capture habit, days 1-7** — lightweight nudges (not streak-shaming) to log short entries — text or voice — tied to moments, not a daily quota.
5. **First weekly reflection, day 7** — delivered in-app and by email: one observation, one tension, one question. This is the core moment of proof. If nothing else in the MVP works, this has to.
6. **Loop continues** — capture, then reflection, on a weekly cadence, referencing earlier entries as the corpus grows.

The entire first-time experience is designed around one goal: get the user to a real, personalized reflection as fast as honestly possible (day 7, not day 30), because that's the moment they decide whether Atlas is worth continuing.

## 3. Main Screens/Pages Required for MVP

- **Onboarding / seed reflection** — one-time guided Q&A
- **Capture screen** — single input box (text + voice toggle), no required fields, no folders or tags
- **Timeline** — reverse-chronological feed of past entries, searchable by keyword
- **Reflections feed** — separate from raw entries; shows weekly (and later monthly) AI-generated reflections as their own artifact, not mixed into the timeline
- **Single entry detail view** — view/edit one entry, see which reflections referenced it
- **Settings** — capture reminders on/off, export data, delete account

That's five screens. No dashboard, no analytics view, no social feed, no goal tracker — resist the urge to add a sixth screen in v1.

## 4. Exact User Flow

Signup → seed reflection → first capture prompt → home screen (defaults to Timeline) → user taps "+" to capture anytime → entries accumulate → on day 7 (and each subsequent 7-day mark), a reflection is generated and surfaces as a notification/email → user opens Reflections feed → reads reflection → optionally taps through to the specific entries it references (link back into Timeline) → user is prompted, softly, to capture again. Search lives in the Timeline and works over entries only, not reflections, for v1.

## 5. Database Entities (High-Level Only)

- **User** — account, auth, subscription status
- **OnboardingProfile** — answers from the seed reflection, treated as a special first-class context object (not just another entry)
- **Entry** — raw captured content (text, transcribed voice), timestamp, optional inferred theme tags (generated by AI, not user-assigned)
- **Reflection** — AI-generated output object; stores the generated text, the time window it covers, and references to the Entry records it drew from
- **ReflectionEntryLink** — join table connecting reflections to the specific entries that informed them, so the "why did it say this" trail is always inspectable
- **CaptureReminderSetting** — per-user notification preferences

Keep this schema small on purpose. No tags-as-first-class-taxonomy, no multi-domain modules (health, relationships, career) yet — those are post-MVP schema decisions, not day-one ones.

## 6. AI Reflection System Logic (Conceptual)

The reflection engine's job is synthesis, not summarization — a good reflection surfaces something the user didn't already know, a bad one just restates their week back to them.

**Inputs:** the OnboardingProfile (stable context: goals, struggles, self-questions), all Entries since the last reflection, and the prior reflection(s) if any exist (so the system can reference continuity — "this is the third time this month...").

**Process (conceptual, not implementation):**
1. Retrieve the relevant window of entries plus the standing profile context.
2. Look across entries for three categories of signal: recurring themes or words, contradictions (stated intent vs. described behavior), and emotional shifts over the window.
3. Draft a reflection that leads with the most surprising or highest-confidence observation first, not a chronological recap.
4. Close with one open question rather than advice — Atlas should provoke reflection, not dispense life coaching or prescriptive fixes. This is a deliberate tone choice: mirror, not mentor.
5. Attach references to the specific entries that informed the observation, so the user can always trace the "why."

**Guardrails:** if there isn't enough signal in a given window (too few entries, too little variation), the reflection should say so honestly rather than manufacture insight — a fabricated-feeling pattern is worse than no reflection at all and will destroy trust fast.

## 7. Success Metrics for the MVP

- **Activation:** % of signups who complete the seed reflection and log at least 3 entries in week 1
- **Reflection open rate:** % of users who open/read the weekly reflection when it arrives — this is the single most important metric in the entire MVP; if this is low, the core hypothesis is failing regardless of anything else
- **Qualitative "would you be disappointed" signal:** direct user interviews after 2-3 reflections, same test used in the pre-build validation phase
- **W4 retention:** % of users still capturing entries 4 weeks after signup
- **Reflection-to-action rate:** % of users who tap through from a reflection back into referenced entries (signals the reflection actually engaged them, not just got glanced at)
- **Paid conversion intent:** in a free/beta phase, % who say yes to "would you pay $15-20/month for this" unprompted, not in response to a discount offer

Vanity metrics to explicitly ignore for now: total entries logged, total words captured, DAU/MAU ratios typical of social apps — none of these tell you whether the reflection is valuable.

## 8. What Would Make Us Kill or Change the Idea

- Reflection open rates stay low (users capture but never read what comes back) — this means the core insight loop isn't landing and no amount of UI polish fixes that.
- Users say the reflections feel generic, obvious, or "like a horoscope" — a clear sign the synthesis isn't actually personalized enough to be worth paying for.
- Retention collapses by week 3-4 at rates worse than typical journaling apps — would suggest Atlas inherited journaling's habit-formation problem without solving it.
- Users explicitly say they'd rather have "a better search over my notes" than "insight about myself" — this would mean we mis-identified the job-to-be-done and should pivot toward a search/retrieval tool instead of a reflection partner.
- A major platform (OpenAI, Apple, Google) ships a comparable proactive-reflection feature inside a free assistant within the validation window — would force an immediate re-evaluation of whether a standalone product is defensible at all.
- Willingness to pay never clears a believable price point in real (not hypothetical) conversations — if nobody will pay $15-20/month after experiencing real reflections, the business model needs to change before more product gets built, not after.
