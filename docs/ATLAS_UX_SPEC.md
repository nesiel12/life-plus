# Atlas UX Spec

Builds on ATLAS_PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md and ATLAS_MVP_PRODUCT_SPEC.md. Covers experience and interface only — no implementation detail.

## 1. Design Philosophy

Atlas should feel like a quiet, perceptive presence — closer to a trusted mentor or a private diary that writes back than to a productivity dashboard. The emotional register is calm, unhurried, and intimate, never urgent, gamified, or performance-driven. This is a deliberate contrast to Notion's "get things done" energy and Day One's "pretty diary" aesthetic — Atlas should feel like the one app in someone's life that isn't asking anything of them except honesty.

The user should move through four emotional states across a session: **safety** (this is a private space, nothing here judges me), **ease** (capturing a thought takes seconds, not a formatted entry), **recognition** (the reflection sounds like it actually knows me), and **gentle challenge** (a well-placed question that lingers after they close the app). If a screen produces anxiety, urgency, or a sense of being evaluated, it's wrong for Atlas — even in later monetization or onboarding flows.

Guiding design principle: **restraint over richness.** Every screen should default to less — less color, less iconography, less decoration — because the emotional weight of the content (someone's real thoughts) should never compete with the interface for attention.

## 2. Complete User Journey

**First minute after signup:** No marketing carousel, no feature tour. A single calm screen states the promise in one line ("Capture a little. Atlas will show you the pattern.") and moves directly into the seed reflection — framed as a conversation, not a form. Typography-led, generous white space, one question visible at a time so it never feels like paperwork.

**First week:** The app should feel almost empty on purpose — a near-blank timeline with one gentle prompt to capture, no streak counters, no red notification badges creating guilt. Every capture should end with quiet acknowledgment (a soft confirmation, never a celebratory animation) — this is a diary, not a game.

**First reflection moment:** This is the emotional peak of the entire product and deserves its own considered arrival — not a push notification competing with fifty other apps, but a distinct, quieter moment: an email and an in-app "your reflection is ready" state that the user chooses to open rather than has interrupted upon them. The reflection itself should read like it was written by someone who was paying close attention, not generated by a template — pacing and whitespace matter as much as the words.

**Long-term usage:** Over months, the emotional arc should shift from novelty to companionship — the product becomes something the user returns to the way they'd return to a trusted conversation, with reflections referencing older reflections so the user feels a continuous relationship rather than a series of disconnected reports.

## 3. MVP Screens

### Onboarding
**Purpose:** Establish trust and seed the AI with real context before any entries exist.
**Main elements:** One question at a time, large serif or humanist type, minimal chrome, subtle progress indicator (dots, not a percentage bar — percentages feel bureaucratic).
**User actions:** Type or speak an answer, skip a question if unsure, move to next.
**Empty state:** N/A — this screen is itself the "empty state" of the product; treat the blank input field as an invitation, with placeholder text that models vulnerability ("I've been feeling stuck on...").
**UX principles:** Never show more than one question at once; never require an answer; conversational tone in copy, not clinical form-field labels.

### Home / Timeline
**Purpose:** A calm, chronological home for everything the user has captured — proof that Atlas is listening, not a productivity feed.
**Main elements:** Reverse-chronological entry list, minimal per-entry preview (timestamp + first line), a persistent but unobtrusive capture affordance, search field.
**User actions:** Scroll, search by keyword, tap into an entry, initiate a new capture.
**Empty state:** Before the first entry, show a single warm prompt and example ("try: 'today I realized...'") rather than an empty-box illustration — illustrations here read as generic SaaS, undermining the intimate tone.
**UX principles:** No unread badges or urgency indicators; entries should feel like they're accumulating patiently, not demanding attention.

### Capture
**Purpose:** Remove every possible reason not to write something down.
**Main elements:** One open text field (auto-expanding), a voice-toggle for speaking instead of typing, no required tags, categories, or titles.
**User actions:** Type, speak, save, discard.
**Empty state:** Not applicable — this screen is always "ready," never populated; the cursor should already be active on open.
**UX principles:** Sub-3-second time-to-first-keystroke; no modal friction (confirmation dialogs, mandatory fields); saving should feel as light as sending a text message.

### Reflection
**Purpose:** Deliver the core value of the product — synthesized insight, not a recap.
**Main elements:** A distinct visual treatment from raw entries (different background tone or typographic treatment signals "this was made for you, not by you"), the reflection text itself, inline links back to the specific entries referenced, a closing open-ended question set apart visually.
**User actions:** Read, tap through to a referenced entry, optionally respond/reflect further, archive or revisit later.
**Empty state:** Before the first reflection exists (days 1-6), show a quiet countdown-free message — not "6 days until your first reflection" (too transactional) but something like "Atlas is paying attention. Your first reflection arrives once there's enough to reflect on."
**UX principles:** Treat each reflection as a small piece of writing worth reading slowly — generous line height, no dense paragraphs, and never truncate with a "read more" pattern that undercuts the emotional weight of the moment.

### Entry Detail
**Purpose:** Let the user revisit a single moment in full, and see how it connected to later insight.
**Main elements:** Full entry text/audio playback, timestamp, list of any reflections that referenced this entry.
**User actions:** Edit, delete, navigate to a linked reflection.
**Empty state:** N/A (only reachable from an existing entry).
**UX principles:** Editing should never feel punitive or bureaucratic — allow quiet correction without version-history anxiety.

### Settings
**Purpose:** Give the user control and reduce anxiety about data and notifications.
**Main elements:** Capture reminder toggle and frequency, data export, account/subscription management, delete-account path that is honest and unhidden.
**User actions:** Toggle reminders, export data, manage subscription, delete account.
**Empty state:** N/A.
**UX principles:** Data control should be immediate and unambiguous — this is sensitive personal content, and trust is built partly through how easy it is to leave or export, not just how easy it is to stay.

## 4. Visual Direction

**Colors:** A restrained, warm-neutral palette — off-whites and warm greys as the dominant surface, one deep, calm accent color (a muted ink blue or deep forest, not a bright SaaS-blue or gradient) used sparingly for the reflection moment specifically, so color itself signals "this is different from your raw notes." Avoid the bright, high-saturation palettes common in productivity apps — they read as energetic, not reflective. Dark mode should feel like dim lamplight, not a coding IDE.

**Typography:** A humanist serif or high-quality serif-adjacent typeface for reflections and long-form reading (signals thoughtfulness, slows reading pace), paired with a clean, neutral sans-serif for UI chrome, timestamps, and navigation. This pairing does real work: it visually distinguishes "what Atlas wrote about you" from "what you wrote yourself" without needing extra labels.

**Layout style:** Generous whitespace, single-column reading-first layouts (no dashboard grids or multi-panel density), soft rounded corners, minimal borders — let spacing, not lines, create structure. Nothing should look like an admin panel or analytics tool.

**Mobile-first approach:** Capture must be effortless one-handed, thumb-reachable, and fast to open — the entire capture flow should be reachable in two taps from any screen. Reflections should be optimized for single-column vertical reading on a phone first, with desktop treated as a wider, more spacious version of the same reading experience rather than a denser one. Voice capture should be a first-class citizen on mobile, not an afterthought, since it's the lowest-friction way to capture a fleeting thought.

Overall design mandate: every visual decision should ask "does this make the user feel like Atlas is paying quiet, careful attention to them" — if a pattern (streaks, badges, bright colors, dense dashboards) borrows from productivity-app conventions instead, cut it, even if it's common elsewhere.
